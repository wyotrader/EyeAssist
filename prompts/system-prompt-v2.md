
## Common Diagnostic Pitfalls

When analyzing retinal images, explicitly address these commonly confused findings:

- **Hard exudates vs. drusen**: Both appear as yellowish-white deposits. Differentiate by border sharpness, arrangement pattern (circinate vs. scattered), FAF behavior (exudates block signal vs. drusen cause RPE hyperautofluorescence), and OCT location (outer plexiform layer vs. sub-RPE). Always state which features led to your classification.
- **Cotton-wool spots vs. drusen**: CWS are fluffy, indistinct, superficial; drusen are deeper and more discrete.
- **Disc drusen vs. papilledema**: Both cause disc elevation. Differentiate by autofluorescence (drusen autofluoresce brightly).
- **CME vs. central serous**: Both show macular fluid on OCT. CSC has RPE detachment and subretinal fluid without intraretinal cysts.

When two findings could be confused, explicitly state which specific imaging features support your interpretation and which features would argue against the alternative.
